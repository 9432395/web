READ THE MARKET · FINAL CHAPTER

Every piece. One single game.

Six chapters, six tools. Now let's play the game: one session read step by step with everything you learned. No magic signals — just reading.

Step 1 · Context: where does the auction wake up?

The day opens inside yesterday's value. The auction's message: agreement, balance. The playbook for the day becomes rotation — buy cheap, sell expensive — until the market proves otherwise (chapters 1 and 2).

Step 2 · Liquidity: price falls toward the value floor

Rotation pushes price down toward the VAL. And right there, the liquidity map shows size waiting: a buy wall defending the floor of value. Passivity already voted — there is real interest in buying that zone. A hypothesis, not a promise (chapter 6).

Step 3 · The footprint: the fight at the low

On top of the wall, sellers try with everything — and the footprint shows the result: heavy aggressive selling, and price stops falling. Absorption. The selling aggression crashes into the buying passivity and loses. The sellers of the low start getting trapped (chapters 3 and 4).

Step 4 · Confirmation: the CVD reveals the exhaustion

One last push scratches a lower low in price — but the CVD prints a higher low. Divergence: sellers push with less force each time. The full story now points to a turn — and you know exactly why (chapter 5).

The game, seen whole

Five reads, one story. The context said balance: rotation between the value extremes. The liquidity showed who defended the floor. The footprint displayed the absorption live. The delta measured the effort without result. And the CVD confirmed the seller exhaustion.

The honest rule: no piece decides alone. Together, they tell the story — and the story is what you trade. Any single tool taken in isolation is just noise with good marketing.

The auction will keep leaving footprints, session after session. Now you can read them. If this course helped you, share it with a trader who needs it — and if you want to go deeper, season 2 covers acceptance vs rejection, day types, and initiative vs responsive activity: pure auction theory.
