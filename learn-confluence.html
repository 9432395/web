READ THE MARKET · SEASON 3 · CHAPTER 6

Where maps agree, pay attention.

Each level family answers a different question — the VWAP speaks for today's average, prior value for yesterday's agreement, the overnight for the night, the IB for the first hour. Most of the time they scatter across the chart. But some days, two or three of them land on the same zone. That is confluence — and it deserves a different level of attention.

Why stacking matters

One level is one group's memory. Two independent levels in the same zone means two different constituencies have a reason to act there — the trader watching yesterday's VAL and the desk benchmarking the weekly VWAP meet at the same price without knowing each other. More independent reasons, more potential participants, more meaningful the reaction. Confluence does not make a level infallible; it makes it informative: whatever happens there, more of the market was watching.

Reading the test with the flow

A confluence zone is where Season 1 comes back on stage. Price arrives — now watch the flow: is the aggression being absorbed (big volume, no progress — someone defending)? Is the attacker exhausting (each push smaller than the last)? Or does it slice through with delta confirming and value migrating behind it? The zone told you where to look. The footprint, the delta and the book tell you what actually happened there.

The honest rule: confluence raises the stakes of a zone, not the certainty of an outcome. Strong zones break too — and when they break with acceptance, that breakout is itself premium information.
